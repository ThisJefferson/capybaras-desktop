    About OpenRouter

    How OpenRouter works, and what it costs

    One account and one key, not a dozen

    OpenRouter is one account and one key that reaches models from many different companies. Without it, you would sign up to each company separately. You do not have to go and create a key yourself — pressing Connect is how you get one.

    What happens when you send a message

    Your message goes to the model you chose, through OpenRouter, and the answer comes back to this window. Capybaras never sees your password: you sign in on OpenRouter’s own page, in your own browser.

    How to connect

    Press Connect. Your browser opens, you sign in to OpenRouter, and you come back to this window. There is nothing to copy and paste.

    The key that comes back is kept by Windows, not in a file the agent could read. You can delete it from your OpenRouter account at any time, and Capybaras will notice rather than fail confusingly.

    How much it costs

    You pay for what you use, by the token. Most replies cost a fraction of a cent, and some models are free. The session panel counts your calls and what they have spent, and the balance line shows what is left.

    Right now, your OpenRouter account has left.

    Why it costs money

    The companies that run the models charge for the computing that answers you. OpenRouter passes that charge through to your account.

    Why the balance says “whole account”

    That figure belongs to your OpenRouter account, not to this app. It is shared with anything else that uses the same account, so it is not a budget Capybaras owns.

    You can read more on OpenRouter’s own page: openrouter.ai.
